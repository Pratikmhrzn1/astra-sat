Overview

What this platform is

NIEC's SAT Prep Platform is a fully custom-built online system designed from the ground up for your students and teachers. It was not adapted from generic software — every feature, every screen, and every workflow was built specifically for how NIEC prepares students for the SAT.

Students can access it from any device at any time — a laptop, phone, or tablet — simply by visiting the website. There is nothing to download or install. When a student logs in, everything they have ever done on the platform is exactly where they left it: their scores, their practice history, their vocabulary progress, everything.

The platform covers the full preparation cycle. On any given day, a student might do a short reading practice set, request AI feedback on the questions they got wrong, review vocabulary words, and check their progress dashboard — all in one place. Teachers can monitor this activity, add new content, write personal feedback, and run live mock exam sessions. Admins have a separate panel to manage accounts and keep the platform running smoothly.

Users

Who the platform serves

The platform has three separate user types. Each one logs into a completely different interface with tools suited to their role. A student never sees the teacher's content manager; a teacher never has access to admin settings.

Student
SAT Aspirants
Students use the platform for all their preparation. They take practice sets, receive AI feedback, do full-length mock exams, study vocabulary, and track their improvement over time. Everything is designed to feel as close to the real SAT as possible.
Teacher
Instructors
Teachers build the content students practice on, monitor individual student performance in detail, write personalised feedback, and run live synchronized mock exams. They have full visibility into what each student is doing and how they are progressing.
Admin
Management
Admins control who can register on the platform and manage all user accounts. They can create access codes for new student batches, deactivate old ones, and download a full backup of all platform data at any time. No technical knowledge is required for any of this.
How new students join

Students cannot simply sign up on their own. Every new account requires a valid access code, which is generated by an admin and given to the student directly. This means the platform remains completely private — only the students NIEC has specifically enrolled can access it. Codes can be set to expire after a certain number of uses, so a code given to one batch of students cannot be used again by a future batch.

Practice

Practice question sets

Practice sets are the core day-to-day preparation tool. They let students work through SAT questions at their own pace, in a focused, distraction-free environment that closely mirrors the real exam.

The SAT has two main sections: Reading & Writing, and Math. The platform has separate question sets for each. Teachers create these sets and publish them when they are ready — students see only the published sets, never the drafts a teacher is still working on.

When a student opens a practice set, they see the same layout they will encounter on the real digital SAT: for reading questions, the passage appears on the left side of the screen and the question on the right. Students can highlight text in the passage, cross out answer options they have ruled out, and flag questions they want to come back to — all features that exist in the official exam software. The goal is to make students feel completely at home on test day.

Timed or untimed

Students can choose whether to practice with a countdown timer running (to build speed under pressure) or without one (to focus on accuracy and understanding). This setting is remembered by the platform, so students do not need to choose it every time. Both modes are valuable at different stages of preparation — untimed for learning concepts, timed for building exam readiness.

After completing a set, the student sees a results page with their score and a breakdown of each question. They can click any question to review it, see what they answered, see the correct answer, and — if they requested it during the session — read their AI feedback. These results are saved permanently and can be revisited from the Results section at any time.

Mock Testing

Adaptive full-length mock exams

The mock exam feature replicates the real SAT experience as closely as possible — including the adaptive scoring system that determines a student's final score on the actual exam.

How the real SAT scoring works

Most students do not know this: the SAT is not a fixed exam. Every student takes the same Module 1 questions. But based on how well they do in Module 1, they are given a different Module 2 — either a harder set or an easier set. This is what makes the SAT "adaptive."

Students who perform well in Module 1 are given the harder Module 2. If they then do well in that harder set, they can reach the highest possible scores. Students who struggle in Module 1 are given the easier Module 2 — which gives them a better chance of showing what they know, but caps the maximum score they can achieve that day. This is not publicly advertised by the College Board, but it is a critical part of how the exam works and how scores are calculated.

How the platform replicates this

The platform's mock test works exactly the same way. A student begins Module 1. When they finish, the platform automatically calculates their score. If they got more than 60% correct, they are assigned the harder Module 2 questions. If they scored below 60%, they receive the easier Module 2 questions. This happens instantly and automatically — the student is simply taken to Module 2 without any interruption.

At the end of the exam, the student sees their combined score and a full breakdown — how they did in each module, which questions they got right and wrong, and where they can improve. This is a meaningful estimate of where they currently stand on the real exam, under realistic conditions.

Why this matters

Many SAT preparation tools give students a set of random practice questions and call it a "mock test." That is not how the real SAT works. Students who practice on a non-adaptive exam are being prepared for a test that does not exist. NIEC's platform prepares students for the actual test they will sit — including the pressure of knowing their Module 1 performance determines the difficulty of what comes next.

Artificial Intelligence

How AI feedback works

The AI feedback system is the most distinctive feature of the platform. When a student asks for help on a question they got wrong, three separate AI checks run simultaneously — producing a complete, personalised explanation within a few seconds.

This is not a simple "here is the correct answer and why" response. The AI reads the specific question, reads the student's stated reasoning, and responds to that student's thinking. Two students who got the same question wrong but for different reasons will receive different feedback. The goal is not to just tell students the answer — it is to help them understand why they thought the way they did and where that thinking went wrong.

The three types of feedback

1. Reasoning Check — runs on every wrong answer

Before a student can request AI feedback, they are asked one question: "What was your thinking when you chose that answer?" The student writes a short explanation in their own words. This short step is important — it forces the student to articulate their logic, which reveals where the misunderstanding actually lives.

The AI then reads both the original question and the student's reasoning, and responds specifically to what that student wrote. If the student misread the passage, the AI addresses that. If the student had the right idea but misapplied it, the AI identifies exactly where the logic broke down. This is the feedback type that runs every single time a student asks for help.

2. Grammar Diagnosis — runs on grammar and writing questions

The Reading & Writing section of the SAT includes questions about punctuation, sentence structure, transitions, and standard English grammar rules. These are some of the most rule-based questions on the exam — a student either knows the rule or they do not.

When a student gets a grammar question wrong, a second AI specifically diagnoses which grammar rule they are breaking. It does not just say the answer is wrong — it names the concept, explains the rule in plain language, and gives the student context for when that rule applies. Over time, this builds a genuine understanding of grammar rather than memorised answer patterns that fall apart under new question formats.

3. Trap Explainer — runs on English multiple-choice questions

One of the most important things students need to understand about the SAT is that it is deliberately designed to mislead. Every wrong answer in a multiple-choice question was carefully written to look plausible to students who are thinking in a certain way. These are called "trap" answers, and they are responsible for a significant portion of the score gap between average and high-scoring students.

When a student picks a wrong answer on an English question, this AI identifies whether the student fell for a trap — and if so, explains exactly how that trap was constructed and why their thinking led them there. Learning to recognise common trap patterns is one of the highest-value skills a student can develop for the SAT, and this feature directly builds that skill through experience.

Speed and convenience

All three feedback types run at the same time, not one after another. The student does not wait for one to finish before the next begins. Within a few seconds of submitting their reasoning, they see a complete, multi-part feedback response covering all three angles simultaneously. There is no "loading" screen, no delay, no interruption to their study flow.

AI-powered question categorisation

There is also an AI working in the background for teachers. When a teacher adds new questions to the platform, they do not need to manually tag each question with its skill type (identifying evidence, vocabulary in context, transitions, standard English conventions, and so on). An AI automatically categorises each question the moment it is saved. This saves teachers a significant amount of manual work and ensures that skill tags are consistent across all content on the platform.

Vocabulary

The vocabulary review system

The SAT requires a strong academic vocabulary. The platform includes a dedicated vocabulary study system built around the most effective known method for long-term word retention: spaced repetition.

Spaced repetition is a learning technique based on the fact that we forget things on a predictable curve. The most effective time to review something is right before you are about to forget it — not immediately after learning it (when it is still fresh) and not weeks later (when it has already been forgotten). By showing a word at exactly the right moment, the memory is strengthened most efficiently.

The platform tracks every vocabulary word for every student individually. Words that a student consistently gets right are shown less frequently — the system trusts that those words are secure. Words a student keeps getting wrong are shown more often — the system focuses effort where it is needed most. The student never has to manage this themselves. They simply open the Vocabulary Review section and study whatever the system presents to them that day.

The format is active recall — the student sees the word and has to retrieve the meaning from memory, rather than reading a definition passively. Research consistently shows that active retrieval is far more effective at building lasting memory than reading a list. Students who use the vocabulary feature consistently will find that high-level academic words they previously found difficult become genuinely familiar over time.

Resources

The resource library

The library is a shared resource space where teachers and admins can upload study materials that all students can access whenever they need them.

This might include official SAT preparation guides, custom notes prepared by NIEC teachers, PDF worksheets, past papers, or links to useful reference materials. Instead of sending materials through WhatsApp or email where they can be lost or hard to find, everything lives in one organised place on the platform.

Students access the library from their navigation menu and can view or download any material that has been uploaded. Teachers and admins can add new materials at any time and remove old ones. There is no limit on the number of files that can be stored.

Teacher Tools

What teachers can do

The teacher portal gives instructors full control over content, student monitoring, personalised feedback, and live examination. Every tool is designed to work without any technical knowledge — teachers work with familiar concepts like forms, lists, and buttons.

Building question content

Teachers add questions through a built-in form. For a reading question, the teacher pastes the passage, types the question, adds the four answer options, and marks which option is correct. For a math question, the same form adapts accordingly. Questions can also be given a difficulty level, which the platform uses to decide which module a question belongs to in the adaptive mock test system.

New question sets are created in "draft" mode. A set in draft is completely invisible to students — the teacher can work on it over several days, adding questions one by one, reviewing them, and editing them, without students ever seeing incomplete content. When the set is ready, the teacher publishes it with a single click, and it immediately becomes available to students.

The platform also auto-saves the teacher's work as they type. If they close the browser accidentally while building a question, their work is still there when they return. Nothing is lost.

Monitoring student performance

The teacher panel shows a list of all students assigned to them. Clicking on any student opens their full profile: every exam they have taken, when they took it, what they scored in each section, and how their scores have changed over time.

Teachers can click into any individual exam result to see a question-by-question breakdown — which questions the student answered correctly, which they got wrong, what they answered, and what the AI told them. This level of detail allows a teacher to have a very specific, informed conversation with a student rather than a general one: "I can see you are consistently misidentifying the author's tone in reading passages — let us work on that."

Writing personalised feedback

After reviewing a student's exam, the teacher can write a personal feedback note directly on that result. This note is sent to the student as a notification — they receive an alert in the platform and can read the feedback from their teacher. This creates a direct feedback loop between teacher and student that goes beyond automated AI responses.

Live exam sessions

The live exam feature allows a teacher to run a synchronized mock test where all participating students start at exactly the same moment. This is designed for simulated test days — where the experience of sitting an exam under real time pressure, with everyone else doing the same thing at the same time, is part of the preparation.

  • 1
    Teacher creates the session
    The teacher selects which English and Math question sets to use for the live exam. The platform generates a unique join code for the session.
  • 2
    Students join the lobby
    Students enter the join code on their device and are taken to a waiting lobby. They can see that they are connected and ready. The teacher can see each student appear in their session panel.
  • 3
    Teacher starts the exam
    When the teacher clicks "Start," all connected students are taken into the exam simultaneously. The timer begins for everyone at the same moment.
  • 4
    Students complete the exam
    Students work through the exam individually on their own device. The teacher can monitor how many students have finished from their panel.
  • 5
    Teacher reviews and releases results
    After the exam, the teacher reviews each student's result one by one, can write personal feedback notes, and then clicks "Release Results" to make them available. Students receive a notification that their results are ready to view.

Admin Panel

What admins can do

The admin panel gives NIEC management oversight and control of the entire platform without needing any technical assistance.

User Management

Admins can see every registered account on the platform — their name, email, role (student, teacher, or admin), when they joined, and which teacher they are assigned to. Accounts can be reviewed and managed at any time. If a student leaves NIEC or a teacher's employment ends, their account can be managed accordingly.

Access Code System

Admins create access codes for each new batch of students. A code can be set to allow only a specific number of registrations — for example, a code for a batch of 30 students will stop working after 30 people have used it. Old codes can be deactivated immediately at any time.

Database Backup

At any time, an admin can download a complete copy of all platform data — every student account, every exam result, every question, every piece of feedback. This backup can be stored securely by NIEC and used to restore the platform in the unlikely event of a technical problem. No data is ever at risk of being lost permanently.

Library Management

Admins can upload, organise, and remove files in the shared resource library. They have the same library management access as teachers, giving them full control over what resources students see.

Email & Notifications

Automated email and in-platform notifications

The platform sends emails automatically when important things happen, and maintains an in-platform notification system so students never miss updates from their teachers.

Welcome email on registration

The moment a new student creates their account, a professionally designed welcome email is sent to their inbox. The email is sent from noreply@mocktest.niec.edu.np — using NIEC's own domain, so it looks professional and familiar rather than coming from an unfamiliar third-party address.

The email contains a record of the student's email address and the password they chose when registering — a convenient reference they can keep for future use. It also links directly to the platform so they can log in immediately.

Password reset emails

Students who forget their password can click "Forgot your password?" on the login page. They enter their email address, and within seconds they receive a password reset email. The email contains a secure link they click to choose a new password. The link expires after one hour for security reasons — if they do not use it in time, they simply request a new one. The entire process takes under two minutes and requires no assistance from a teacher or admin.

In-platform notifications for students

When a teacher releases results from a live exam, the affected students receive an in-platform notification. A banner appears the next time they open the platform alerting them that new results are available. Similarly, if a teacher writes personal feedback on one of their exam results, the student is notified. This ensures important communications are never missed.

AI System

The AI models powering the platform

The platform uses two AI models, both from Anthropic — one of the world's leading AI companies, known for building AI that is reliable, accurate, and safe. The two models are different strengths of the same family: one is optimised for speed and quick tasks, the other for depth and quality.

Quick Feedback
Claude Haiku 4.5
Haiku is Anthropic's fast, efficient model. It is used for per-question AI feedback — the reasoning checks, grammar diagnosis, and trap explanations that students receive when they get a question wrong. Speed matters here: students should not be waiting. Haiku responds within a couple of seconds, keeping the study flow uninterrupted. It is also the most cost-efficient model, which matters because it is used very frequently.

Deep Analysis
Claude Sonnet 4.6
Sonnet is Anthropic's more capable model. It is used for tasks that require deeper reasoning and higher accuracy — specifically, the automatic categorisation of teacher-uploaded questions by skill type, and the generation of richer narrative feedback. These tasks run less frequently than per-question feedback, and quality matters more than raw speed, so Sonnet is the right choice.

What "per million words" means in practice

These prices sound large but the numbers involved are very small. "One million words" is roughly equivalent to the entire Harry Potter series five times over. A single AI feedback response — reading a question and a student's reasoning, then producing an explanation — uses approximately 300–600 words total. At Haiku's pricing, that costs less than one-tenth of a cent.

For a realistic estimate: if 100 students each complete two practice sessions per week and request AI feedback on an average of 6 wrong answers per session, that is roughly 4,800 feedback requests per month. The total AI cost for that level of activity would be approximately $5 to $15 per month. Even with 300 very active students, total monthly AI costs would be unlikely to exceed $50. You pay only for what is used — there is no AI cost on any day when no students are active.

Technology Partners

Yeti Host is a Nepal-based cloud hosting provider. The SAT platform runs on a dedicated virtual server on their infrastructure. Access & Security

How the platform is accessed and kept secure

The platform is designed to be easy for authorised users to access and effectively locked to everyone else.

Invite-only registration

Nobody can create an account on the platform without a valid access code. Access codes are generated exclusively by NIEC admins and given directly to students. This means there is no risk of unauthorised users finding the website and creating accounts. The platform is as private as NIEC wants it to be.

Encrypted passwords

All passwords are stored using industry-standard encryption. This means that even if someone were to access the database, they could not read any user's password. Each password is converted into a scrambled code that cannot be reversed.

Automatic account lockout

If someone tries to log into an account with the wrong password repeatedly, the platform automatically locks that account for 15 minutes. This prevents brute-force attacks — where someone tries thousands of password combinations to gain access to an account.

Session security

When a user logs in, their session is secured with a time-limited token that expires automatically. If a user leaves the platform open on a shared device and someone else tries to use it later, they are redirected to the login screen rather than getting access to the first user's account.

HTTPS encryption

All communication between a student's device and the platform is encrypted via HTTPS. This is the same standard used by banks and secure websites. Any data sent or received — including exam answers, login credentials, and personal information — cannot be intercepted by a third party.

Works on all devices

Students can access the platform from any device with a modern browser — a Windows laptop, a Mac, an Android phone, or an iPhone. Nothing needs to be installed. The interface adapts to the screen size so it is readable and usable regardless of device.
